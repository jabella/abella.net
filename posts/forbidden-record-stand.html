Forbidden Record Stand

I want a record stand that I can’t have — or, how I learned to pirate hardware.

Specifications

Build time
6 hours over a few weekends
Materials
3/4″ birch plywood
Tools
Fusion 360, outsourced CNC
Cost
$420
Status
Done

An absolutely perfect record stand that fits in my space is only sold in New Zealand. You can’t get it in America. I needed it.

It looked easy enough, but I want it exact. I didn’t want some squared off, basic-looking thing. I want this shelf.

The first problem

If I’m going to build my own, I have to come up with dimensions and drawings for it. The trouble is I don’t know how to do any of that in modern CAD software.

Learning to use the tools to make the thing you want is half the fun, right?

Thankfully the vendor posted some rough dimensions, and we know how large an album cover is and can make some inferences between that and the thickness of the wood. I fired up Fusion and spent my lunch hour watching tutorials and fumbling for days.

Off to be cut

With the model done, I exported the panels as DXF, nested them onto a single sheet, and sent them off to a shop to cut. Then came the hard part: waiting.

It fit the first try

Considering how little information I had to go on, it all came out great. I changed the design in a few ways to fit my space better — a record flip bin on the left, turntable on the right — and despite it being my first ever CAD work for something like this, it all fit the first try.

Turns out the surest way to get the thing you can’t have is to learn how to make it — and mine fits better anyway.
